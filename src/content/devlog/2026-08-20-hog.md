Today's devlog will be a little different. I completed the battle mode rework based on the new combat pipeline and updated all of the abilities and Biograms, which just brings us back to parity so it's not much to look at right now. Instead, I thought it would be fun to talk about my history with making games, so let's try that out and see how it goes.

---

One day in 2004, a friend of mine showed me how to program text-based adventure games on a TI-83 graphing calculator. In 2006, another friend of mine told me about this computer science class he had taken and how cool it was. And in 2007, I learned Java in that computer science class and programmed a small text-based version of RuneScape that I called QuestScape. In 2010, I had to write a program using Java Swing as the final project for the class. It was open-ended, so I decided to make an interactive version of QuestScape. It was, unsurprisingly, really hard to explain what it was I built to a class where no one knew what RuneScape was.

![QuestScape-Swing](/devlog/history-of-games/questscape.png)

Over the next couple of years, I would build several random Java applications whenever I was bored and had some free time. Then, in December 2011, I started work on what I consider my first real game. I had just learned about roguelikes and wanted to build one to really expand my skills as a programmer. I didn't have much of a plan early on for what the game was; I just liked seeing my creativity come to life in a way I had never been able to do before. One day in 2012, I had come up with the name [StoneQuest](/stonequest) and later, a story about delving into dwarvish mountains and uncovering ancient artifacts and the evil within. 

![StoneQuest](/devlog/history-of-games/stonequest.png)

In 2014, I joined UT's new [GAMMA program](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/news/tags/gamma) for game design. The first class I took, I worked with two other students to build a roguelike in C++ using the [Ogre3D](https://www.ogre3d.org/) game engine, which we named ROgre.

![ROgre](/devlog/history-of-games/rogre.png)

The following semester I took the GAMMA capstone class where I teamed up with four other students with backgrounds in art, sound, and production. Together, we made [After](/after), a game about what happens after the world ends. It's a small puzzle adventure game built in Unity (about 15-20 mins of play time). 

![After](/devlog/history-of-games/after.png)

In 2015, I began working full-time at a local startup and, together with my coursework, I didn't have time to work on games anymore. I continued my career into big tech for the next decade until March 2024 when I committed myself to building a game again. At first, I didn't intend for it to become a real game. It was meant to be a quick exercise to just enjoy programming and prove to myself that I could still make stuff. But somewhere along the way, I began to feel passion again; the same that I felt when I was working on StoneQuest all those years ago. That game became [Script Kitties](/script-kitties). Since then, every weekend and bit of spare time has been devoted to working on this game. Then, in June 2026, I finally chose to leave big tech to focus on Script Kitties full-time.

![Script Kitties](/devlog/history-of-games/script-kitties.png)

And that brings us to now. I'm finishing up a playtest demo which will showcase the core game mechanics and I'm both anxious and excited to see what people who aren't friends and family think. That'll be in a future update within the next few weeks, so keep an eye out for that if you're interested.
